# Almond Pruning and Orchard Residue Logistics Guide

Note: this is a synthetic demonstration document shipped with sci-rag-kit.
Its numbers are plausible but fictional.

## 1 Residue types

Almond orchards produce three distinct residue streams: annual prunings
from routine canopy maintenance, whole-tree biomass at orchard removal
(typically year 25), and hulls and shells from processing. This guide
covers field residues: prunings and orchard removal wood.

## 2 Annual pruning yields

| Orchard age | Pruning yield (dry tons/acre/yr) | Typical moisture at chipping (%) |
|-------------|---------------------------------:|---------------------------------:|
| 3 to 7 years | 0.4 | 42 |
| 8 to 15 years | 0.8 | 38 |
| 16 to 25 years | 1.1 | 35 |

Mature blocks in the demo region average 0.9 dry tons per acre per year
of prunings. Whole-orchard removal yields 28 to 34 dry tons per acre as a
one-time pulse.

## 3 Field operations

Prunings are pushed to windrows within two weeks of cutting and chipped
directly into chip trailers. A two-machine crew (loader plus drum
chipper) averages 22 dry tons per hour on windrowed prunings. Chipping
and roadside costs in 2023 averaged 19 dollars per dry ton; highway
transport added 0.32 dollars per dry ton-mile.

## 4 Storage behavior

Chip piles stored uncovered lose 1.5 to 2 percent dry matter per month in
winter and heat internally above 55 degrees Celsius in the first three
weeks. Piles intended for conversion feedstock should turn over within 90
days. Screened overs (greater than 3 inches) should be re-chipped before
gasification to keep feed handling stable.

## 5 End uses

Chipped prunings in the demo region split between soil incorporation (48
percent), co-generation fuel (31 percent), and emerging conversion
projects including gasification to biochar (21 percent). Gasifiers
accept orchard chips at up to 20 percent moisture, so prunings chipped
green usually require field drying or forced-air drying before conversion.
