# Anaerobic Digestion of Crop Residues: A Working Primer

Note: this is a synthetic demonstration document shipped with sci-rag-kit.
Its numbers are plausible but fictional.

## 1 Why digestion suits wet, high-ash residues

Anaerobic digestion converts biomass to biogas in sealed, oxygen-free
tanks using mixed microbial communities. Because the process is wet, it
tolerates feedstocks that combust poorly, including rice straw with its
18 percent ash and high silica. Digestion also preserves nutrients in the
digestate, which returns to fields as a soil amendment.

## 2 Typical biogas yields

| Feedstock | Biogas yield (cubic meters per dry ton) | Methane content (%) |
|-----------|----------------------------------------:|--------------------:|
| Rice straw (alkali pretreated) | 320 | 54 |
| Rice straw (untreated) | 210 | 52 |
| Almond hulls | 380 | 56 |
| Food processing waste | 520 | 60 |

Untreated rice straw digests slowly; a mild alkali soak (2 percent sodium
hydroxide, 24 hours) raises biogas yield by roughly 50 percent by
loosening lignin's grip on cellulose.

## 3 Process conditions

Demo-region digesters run mesophilic (37 degrees Celsius) with hydraulic
retention times of 25 to 35 days for straw-heavy diets. Solids loading
above 12 percent total solids requires mechanical mixing or plug-flow
designs. Co-digesting straw with nitrogen-rich substrates keeps the
carbon-to-nitrogen ratio in the favorable 20-to-30 range; straw alone
runs nitrogen-poor at about 60.

## 4 From biogas to products

Raw biogas at 52 to 60 percent methane can fire boilers directly, run
engine generators, or be upgraded to renewable natural gas by removing
carbon dioxide and hydrogen sulfide. Upgrading typically becomes
economic above 250 standard cubic feet per minute of raw biogas
production.

## 5 Siting logic

Digesters favor locations within a 25-mile haul of dense residue supply.
In the demo region that logic points to the Colusa Basin for rice straw
and the processing corridor for almond hulls, with the Maxwell district
frequently cited as the strongest co-location of straw supply and gas
pipeline access.
