## 1 Summary

The Colusa Basin remains the densest rice straw resource in the demo
region. Across the 2023 season, growers harvested 141,000 acres of rice,
generating an estimated 302,000 dry tons of rice straw after accounting
for straw incorporated back into soils. Roughly 88,000 dry tons were
baled and removed from fields, with the remainder chopped and
incorporated during winter flooding.

## 3 Seasonality and access

Rice straw becomes available in a narrow window between harvest and the
onset of winter rains, typically the last week of September through early
November. Fields converted to winter waterfowl habitat are unavailable
for baling after flood-up. Baling operations in 2023 averaged 11 working
days per district before rain closures.

## 4 Quality

Composite bale samples showed a moisture content of 12 to 16 percent at
roadside, ash content near 18 percent (high silica, typical for rice
straw), and a higher heating value of 5,900 Btu per dry pound. High ash
and silica limit direct combustion and favor biological conversion routes
such as anaerobic digestion.

## 5 Competing uses

About 21,000 dry tons of baled straw went to erosion control and dairy
bedding in 2023. Contracts for those uses set an effective floor price of
34 dollars per delivered ton that any conversion project must beat.
