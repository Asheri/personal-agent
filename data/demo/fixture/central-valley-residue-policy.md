# Demo Region Policy Landscape for Agricultural Residue Utilization

Note: this is a synthetic demonstration document shipped with sci-rag-kit.
Its numbers are plausible but fictional and its programs are invented.

## 1 Burning restrictions

Open burning of orchard prunings has been phased down since 2020 under
the demo region's Agricultural Burning Reduction Rule, with a full
prohibition for orchards larger than 40 acres taking effect in June
2025. The rule is the single strongest driver pushing prunings toward
chipping, soil incorporation, or energy recovery.

## 2 Incentive programs

The Residue Utilization Credit (RUC) pays conversion facilities 18
dollars per dry ton of qualifying crop residue diverted from open burning
or field decomposition, capped at 40,000 tons per facility per year.
Qualifying feedstocks include orchard prunings, rice straw, and nut
shells; dairy manure is covered by a separate program.

A companion Equipment Modernization Grant covers up to 35 percent of the
purchase price of chippers, balers, and forced-air drying equipment for
operators who commit residues to a registered conversion facility for at
least five years.

## 3 Carbon accounting

Projects claiming the RUC must report lifecycle greenhouse gas
performance annually. Biochar applied to soils may claim durable carbon
storage at 2.6 tons of carbon dioxide equivalent per ton of biochar,
subject to a 100-year permanence discount schedule. Renewable natural gas
from digesters registers under the demo region's clean fuel standard,
where straw-derived gas earned an average credit value of 61 dollars per
metric ton of carbon dioxide equivalent in 2023.

## 4 Permitting notes

Digesters below 500,000 gallons of working volume follow a streamlined
county-level permit path with a target decision time of 120 days.
Gasifiers are permitted as minor stationary sources when potential
emissions stay below 10 tons per year of any criteria pollutant; walnut
shell units in the demo region have consistently qualified.

## 5 Outlook

Program budgets fund the RUC through 2028. Stakeholder workshops in 2024
signaled intent to raise the per-ton credit for high-silica residues such
as rice straw, acknowledging their weaker markets and higher handling
cost.
