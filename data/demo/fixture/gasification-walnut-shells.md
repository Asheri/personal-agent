# Small-Scale Gasification of Walnut Shells and Orchard Chips

Note: this is a synthetic demonstration document shipped with sci-rag-kit.
Its numbers are plausible but fictional.

## 1 Overview

Downdraft gasifiers in the 100 to 500 kilowatt range have run reliably on
walnut shells in the demo region since 2019. Shells arrive from
processors at 8 to 10 percent moisture, already sized between one quarter
and one inch, which makes them close to an ideal gasifier fuel with
minimal preprocessing.

## 2 Fuel specifications

| Property | Walnut shells | Almond orchard chips |
|----------|--------------:|---------------------:|
| Moisture (%, as received) | 9 | 35 |
| Ash (%) | 1.1 | 2.8 |
| Higher heating value (Btu/dry lb) | 8,500 | 8,100 |
| Acceptable as delivered? | yes | requires drying to 20% |

Orchard chips must be dried to at most 20 percent moisture before
gasification; wet chips collapse gas quality and drive tar formation.

## 3 Products and yields

At reference conditions a downdraft unit converts one dry ton of walnut
shells into approximately 1.1 megawatt hours of net electricity plus 180
pounds of biochar. Biochar from shells carries 78 percent fixed carbon
and qualifies for soil amendment markets, which paid 480 dollars per ton
in 2023 demo-region contracts.

## 4 Operations notes

Bridging in the fuel hopper is the leading cause of unplanned outages;
shell fines below one eighth inch should stay under 6 percent of the fuel
mix. Grate temperature above 950 degrees Celsius accelerates clinker
formation with high-ash fuels, which is why rice straw is not fired in
these units.

## 5 Supply picture

Regional walnut processing generates about 41,000 dry tons of shells per
year, of which roughly 15,000 tons remain uncommitted after longstanding
poultry bedding and abrasives contracts. New projects should secure shell
supply agreements before the September processing season begins.
